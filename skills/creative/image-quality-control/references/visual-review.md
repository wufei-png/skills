# Visual review

Review actual artwork; do not convert this guide into a mandatory numerical score.

## At thumbnail size

- Is the subject, action, or spatial mechanism readable without the title?
- Do silhouette, large shapes, color relationships, and light establish a clear focal hierarchy?
- Does the work retain the selected reference's medium and appeal?

## At original resolution

- Are bright accents concentrated where intended, with quiet material regions between them?
- Do fine marks explain the material or merely repeat across unrelated surfaces?
- Are eye catchlights, reflective edges, waterlines, caustics, foam, stars, dry-brush gaps, and deliberate grain preserved where appropriate?
- For figures, objects and architecture, are visible anatomy, contacts, openings, depth and scale coherent enough for the artwork's intended reading?

## When comparing a correction

- Compare the full images and matching native-resolution material details.
- Check for changes to identity, composition, color, light paths, silhouette, or important negative spaces.
- Look for plastic smoothing, lost transparency, weak volume, or mechanically spaced highlight bands.
- Treat a reduction in visible small marks as useful only when the artwork improves or retains its intended appeal.
- If a targeted prompt also changes other regions, record the drift; do not claim exact pixel preservation.

## Record a concise outcome

Use the existing collection schema where available. Record concrete observations and, when relevant and known, selection decisions, limitations, and user review status. Suitable observations describe what is visible, such as a clean glass interior with a preserved waterline, or calmer sea highlights that have become too evenly arranged.
